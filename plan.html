The seven steps

1

Send the same scope to four firms, not a description of the problem

Write one page: address, what is stuck, that access from above has failed, that below-access through the storage-unit ceiling may be required, that the unit is occupied, and that you want one contract covering plumbing and drywall. Send it unchanged to the four B + C-36 firms and to whoever quoted you before. Identical scope is what makes bids comparable.

Source: CSLB — get at least three written bids on identical scope

2

Ask five questions on the phone before anyone visits

Answers should be immediate and specific. A firm that has done this work will describe the access route without prompting.

Source: CSLB — verifying a contractor

3

Verify the licence number that appears on the written bid

Check status, classification, bond and workers' compensation on the official CSLB record, and read the complaint-disclosure section if one is present. The number on the bid must match the number on the record and the number in the company's advertising.

Source: CSLB — check a licence

4

Require a written scope with the drywall priced separately

The quote should itemise the plumbing work, the ceiling opening, the patch and finish, protection of the tenant's belongings, and disposal. Vague “drywall included” language is how a $600 job becomes a $2,100 conversation.

Source: Angi — drywall adds about $2,100 when a wall is opened (updated 2026-09-18)

5

Agree the access plan, the tenant terms and a stop-and-report point

Confirm where the opening will be cut, how big, how it will be covered while open, the working hours, the notice given to the tenant, and what happens if hidden damage is found. Work beyond the agreed scope should require your approval and a photograph before it starts.

Source: Angi — water-damage repair $1,350–$6,100 (updated 2026-10-28)

6

Put the contract and payment terms in writing

CSLB guidance: a licensed contractor must give you a written contract on a home improvement project, and cannot ask for a down payment of more than 10% of the contract price or $1,000, whichever is less, unless a blanket performance and payment bond is on file. Both insurance types must be described in the contract. Confirm certificates of insurance before work starts.

Source: CSLB — down-payment limit and insurance requirements

7

Verify on site, then verify the finish

On the day: match the licence on the truck and business card to CSLB, and confirm the people attending are covered by the insurance you were shown. After the work: test the trip lever and the overflow, confirm the ceiling is patched to the agreed finish, photograph everything before the opening is closed, and keep the signed scope with the invoice.

Source: CSLB — verify licence and insurance at the point of hire

Five questions to ask on the phone

  1. Have you freed a seized trip lever inside a concealed waste-and-overflow assembly without opening the wall — and if not, what is your plan?
  2. Will you open the storage-unit ceiling, and who patches and finishes it afterwards?
  3. Which licence number will appear on the written bid, and what classifications does it carry?
  4. Who will be on site, and are they covered by your workers' compensation insurance?
  5. How do you protect an occupied unit — dust barrier, floor covering, notice, hours, clean-up?

A firm that has done this work will answer the first question with an access plan, not a caveat. If the answer is “we will see when we get there”, that is the answer.

Scope checklist — send this with the request

Ten lines. Every one of them should appear, priced or explicitly excluded, in the written quote.

  • Access route stated: from above, from below, or a two-stage attempt
  • Size and location of any opening, and who cuts it
  • Plumbing: adjust, replace the trim, or replace the waste-and-overflow assembly — stated explicitly
  • Drywall: patch, tape, sand and paint to an agreed finish level, priced as its own line
  • Protection: floor coverings, dust barrier, covering of stored items in the storage unit
  • Tenant terms: notice period, working hours, access to the unit and to the storage unit
  • Disposal of removed material
  • Stop-and-report clause: hidden damage inspected, photographed and approved before extra work
  • Payment schedule within the CSLB down-payment limit
  • Insurance certificates for liability and workers' compensation attached to the contract

Contract and payment terms

  • A licensed contractor must provide a written contract on a home improvement project, and the licence number must appear on the contract and on all advertising. CSLB guidance
  • Down payment: no more than 10% of the contract price or $1,000, whichever is less, unless the contractor has a blanket performance and payment bond on file with CSLB. CSLB — down-payment limit
  • The contract must describe both workers'-compensation and liability insurance. Ask for the certificates and check the named entity matches the licence. CSLB — insurance
  • Licensing threshold: the law does not apply below $1,000 aggregate contract price and only where the work does not require a building permit (BPC §7048). BPC §7048

Still open before you book: which entity quoted you, whether the Yelp listing matches the licence, and which firm has actually done concealed-access work in an occupied unit. Those are the flagged items.
