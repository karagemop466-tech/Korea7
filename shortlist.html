Shortlist — who can do the plumbing and the drywall

The job needs one firm for a delicate repair in occupied space, with a licensed plumber and the ability to open and restore the ceiling below. The first list prioritizes contractors whose own CSLB license carries both the plumbing (C-36) and the general-building (B) classification; this is not the only possible arrangement. No company has confirmed this specific scope. Every license value below was read from the official CSLB record on 2026-11-07; every row links to its official source. Where a value could not be confirmed from an official source it is left blank and flagged.

1. Candidates with verified B General Building + C-36 Plumbing classifications

Why this matters: CSLB's B classification text states a general building contractor “shall not take a prime contract for any project involving trades other than framing or carpentry unless … the general building contractor holds the appropriate license classification or subcontracts with an appropriately licensed specialty contractor.” Dual classifications are a useful screening signal, not confirmation of job-specific scope or availability. A specialty contractor may also undertake other work that is incidental and supplemental to its licensed work; whether a particular ceiling repair qualifies depends on the facts. Confirm the scope and responsible trades in writing. CSLB — B classification · California BPC §7059.

4. What to ask each firm on the first call

  1. “Will you attempt the overflow from above first, and what happens if the linkage is seized?” Ask for the fallback in writing before anyone opens anything.
  2. “Where exactly do you cut the ceiling, how big is the opening, and who patches and finishes it?” One answer, one invoice — the reason the B + C-36 firms are shortlisted.
  3. “Will you fill the tub and inspect the overflow and drain joints under flow before the ceiling is closed?” A water test is the standard check on a waste-and-overflow assembly.
  4. “Who will be on site, and what is your workers'-compensation coverage for them?” Three shortlisted records show a “no employees” exemption — confirm coverage for the actual crew.
  5. “Give me the CSLB license number on the contract and the written scope.” Then re-check it the same day at cslb.ca.gov → Check a License and read the CSLB hiring guidance.
  6. “How will you protect the tenant's space and schedule the work?” Containment, water shut-off window, debris removal, and a start/finish time in writing.

5. Permit question (neutral, official sources only)

San Francisco's plumbing permit guidance states that a permit is required before cutting into or replacing pipes, particularly pipes covered by a wall, and lists the license types DBI accepts on a plumbing permit. The local exemptions are narrow — e.g. “repair of leaks not requiring cutting into or removing piping.” Which category the final scope falls into is a question for the contractor and DBI, not something this site can certify.
