Permits & licensing — official rules

San Francisco permit requirements and California contractor-licensing rules, quoted from official government pages with links. This page summarizes public rules only and is not legal advice.

1. Does this work need a San Francisco permit?

San Francisco's Department of Building Inspection (DBI) guidance on sf.gov — "Apply for a plumbing and mechanical permit" states:

"You need a permit before cutting into or replacing pipes, particularly pipes that will be covered by a wall or buried in the ground. See exemptions in San Francisco Plumbing Code Section 104.2."

The exemptions referenced there mirror California Plumbing Code §104.2 ("Exempt Work"), which the code publisher (IAPMO) annotates as follows (IAPMO code spotlight — publisher reference, not SF-specific text):

  1. Stopping leaks in drains, soil, waste or vent pipe — but if a concealed trap, drainpipe, soil, waste or vent pipe must be removed and replaced with new material, that "shall be considered as new work and a permit shall be procured and inspection made."
  2. Clearing stoppages / repairing leaks in pipes, valves or fixtures — provided the repairs do not involve replacement or rearrangement of valves, pipes or fixtures.

2. Who may legally do the work (California license rules)

  • Small-job exemption — current law: Business & Professions Code §7048 exempts work only when the aggregate contract price is under $1,000, the work is of a "casual, minor, or inconsequential nature," and it does not require a building permit (plus further limits on advertising and employing others). Text as amended by AB 1170 (Stats. 2025, ch. 67), effective January 1, 2026: leginfo.legislature.ca.gov — BPC §7048.
  • Why many sources say "$500": that was the pre-2026 threshold. Third-party pages still citing $500 are out of date — see flag F12.
  • Effect here: the replacement scope is permit-type plumbing work, so the §7048 exemption would not shield an unlicensed handyman for it; a CSLB-licensed contractor is required — matching the owner's own requirement.

Practical read: a firm holding B + C-36 (Ace Plumbing & Rooter #829071; General SF #959843 — both verified active) can sign one contract covering the ceiling opening, the plumbing repair/replacement, and the drywall patch. A C-36-only plumber can lawfully do the plumbing and hire/subcontract the drywall; the owner then manages two scopes. A B-2 firm could take a multi-trade remodel package but cannot itself replace the concealed plumbing unless it holds C-36 or subcontracts it.

3. Before signing — checklist

  1. Re-verify the license live on CSLB the day of hire (links in the provider table).
  2. Confirm the license classification covers the contracted scope (B + C-36 for a combined contract; C-36 alone if drywall is subcontracted).
  3. Request: certificate of liability insurance, workers'-compensation certificate (note the two CSLB insurance flags F5–F6), and a written scope separating plumbing and drywall line items.
  4. Review the official rules with eyes open (Section 1 and flag F4).
  5. Agree in writing on tenant access windows, water-shutoff timing, dust protection for the storage room, and patch/finish level.
