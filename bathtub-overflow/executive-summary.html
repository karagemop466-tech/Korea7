Executive summary

Compiled 2026-11-06 from official sources (CSLB, sf.gov, California legislative text) plus the companies' own websites and Yelp listings. A plain-text copy is in the repository at EXECUTIVE-SUMMARY.md.

The problem

The bathtub's wastewater overflow trip lever is stuck and the lever/overflow assembly sits in concealed plumbing behind the bathroom wall of a tenant-occupied unit in San Francisco. Because the overflow opening is the only practical access point from the tub side, plumbers who examined it (owner reports 5+ licensed plumbers) were unwilling to force removal from above for fear of destroying the hidden plumbing. Their recommendation (owner-reported) is to work from below, which means opening the drywall ceiling of the storage unit directly underneath. The owner therefore needs a single service that can do both the plumbing and the drywall opening/patch, in an occupied unit, done carefully.

Client-reported The occupancy, the 5+ failed from-above attempts, and the storage room below are owner-provided facts and have no official source; they are the basis of the requirements, not findings.

Key verified findings

  1. Two of the six verified candidates can lawfully do the plumbing and the drywall on one contract. CSLB records show Ace Plumbing & Rooter (#829071) and General SF (#959843) hold both B — General Building and C-36 — Plumbing classifications, both active as of 2026-11-06. The other four verified/quoted companies hold C-36 (plumbing) only and would need a drywall subcontractor or a second contract.
  2. San Francisco's official permit guidance covers this exact scenario. SFDBI: "You need a permit before cutting into or replacing pipes, particularly pipes that will be covered by a wall or buried in the ground" (sf.gov). The code exemptions (SF Plumbing Code §104.2, mirroring CPC §104.2) cover stopping leaks and clearing stoppages only when no concealed pipe is removed and replaced; replacing concealed material "shall be considered as new work and a permit shall be procured." The planned work therefore splits: a linkage adjustment may be exempt; replacing the concealed waste-and-overflow assembly matches the permit-required description.
  3. The owner currently plans to proceed without a permit this week. This is the owner's decision and is recorded — with the official rules and without any private circumstances — on the Flagged for review page (item F4) for explicit sign-off before work starts. Hiring a licensed, bonded, insured contractor (as the owner requires) is unaffected by that decision.
  4. California's handyman licensing exemption changed on 2026-01-01. B&P Code §7048, as amended by AB 1170 (Stats. 2025, ch. 67), exempts only jobs under $1,000 that do not require a building permit (plus other conditions). Widely cited older "$500" figures are out of date. Because the replacement scope is plumbing-permit work, the exemption would not apply to it; a CSLB-licensed contractor in the right classification is required — which the owner wants anyway.
  5. Occupied-unit/property-manager experience is verifiable for three candidates from their own official sites: Fast Response (HOA condos, large apartment buildings, property managers), Bay Area Plumbing (landlords/property managers/HOAs page), Ace Plumbing (HOAs & Property Managers page). For the others this cell is left blank rather than inferred.

Recommendation

  1. Shortlist the dual-classified licensees first: Ace Plumbing & Rooter (#829071 — active, bonded, workers' comp on file) and General SF (#959843 — active; note CSLB shows a workers'-comp exemption "no employees," so require proof of insurance and clarify crewing — flag F6). A single B + C-36 prime can open the ceiling, repair the waste-and-overflow/trip-lever plumbing, and patch the drywall under one contract and one point of accountability.
  2. Get the scope in writing before work: ceiling opening size and location, plumbing scope (adjust vs. replace assembly), patch/finish level, cleanup, and tenant-access protocol. Confirm in writing whether the price includes the drywall work.
  3. Re-verify each license the day of hire via the CSLB links in the provider table, and request a certificate of liability insurance plus the workers'-comp certificate.
  4. Decide the permit posture explicitly after reading the official rules on the Permits & licensing page and the flag (F4).
  5. Sequence: schedule the storage room below for the ceiling opening, coordinate date/time with tenants, plan water shutoff windows, and keep the fallback (assembly replacement from below) priced in the same written scope so no second mobilization is needed if the adjustment attempt fails.
