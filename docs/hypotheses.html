Five hypotheses, ranked, with outcomes

Each entry names the layer(s), the physical signature, why it should catch a fault missing from the catalogue rather than one already in it, and how it differs from what this programme has already done. Rank is expected DTI gain per unit implementation cost, assessed before any experiment was run. Two were tested this session, one was tested and abandoned, one was not run because its inputs are already inside the organisers' 19 bands, and one is blocked on data.

H5 — 1 m lidar scarp detection (rank 1, BLOCKED)

  • Layers: USGS 3DEP 1 m lidar DEM tiles, listed for competitors in the organisers' 1m_DEM_links.csv.
  • Signature: curvature of the detrended 1 m surface, and the base-of-slope break of a free face; the classic scarp morphology operator, run at 1 m and then the sharpest response sampled onto the 100 m grid.
  • Why blind faults: the catalogue's mapped traces are, by construction, the ones someone drew. A scarp that is visible in 1 m lidar but absent from the map is precisely the profile of an expert-identified new fault. At 100 m posting — the resolution of training_features.tif — a 2–4 m scarp is one pixel of smoothing; at 1 m it is 200+ pixels of coherent signal. This is the single largest resolution gap available and it is free and official.
  • Differs because: no submission in the programme has used sub-100 m data. Every one of the nine focus files is a thresholded version of the same 100 m stack.
  • Blocker, verified: the DEM tiles are on OpenTopography / 3DEP, tens of GB, and the competition's link file is login-walled. Neither is reachable from this sandbox. This is recorded as the highest-value next step, not as a result.

H2 — Coverage-first emission with seismicity modulation (rank 2, TESTED, ADOPTED)

  • Layers: none for the geometry; INGENIOUS independent (declustered) earthquake rate density, variant ieq_n200a15, 500 m, warped to the competition grid.
  • Signature: an 11 px lattice (coverage), with nodes dropped where the seismogenic lineament field is in its lowest 30%, and every node within 3 px of a catalogue trace removed.
  • Why blind faults: a lattice asserts nothing about which fault; it maximises the chance that some dot lands within 300 m of any structure at all. Because β=0.8 weights missed truth four times more heavily than false-positive mass, coverage is the correct objective when a ranking surface has little skill. The seismicity only breaks ties.
  • Differs because: the programme has always optimised concentration (thin a ridge, then prune), never coverage. The one coverage attempt that exists — the 5 px lattice, reported 0.0904 — was a bare lattice with no modulation. This is the only file in the programme whose dot set is near-orthogonal to all nine focus rasters (max 3 px overlap 16.9%, |ρ| 0.006).
  • Outcome: battery 0.0463 on the holdout instrument against 0.0405 for uniform random at the same 20,000-dot budget (+14% relative, non-overlapping CIs). Adopted for the generated file. MODEL-ESTIMATE 0.0427. It clears uniqueness and format; it fails the score gate.

H1 — Seismogenic lineament field as a selector (rank 3, TESTED, NEGATIVE)

  • Layers: same declustered rate density, plus a 4 px Gaussian and a six-orientation Hessian ridge response.
  • Signature: the ridge response is large where the epicentre density forms a line rather than a blob: the classical seismological signature of a fault. Gradients of the rate give structure-boundary magnitude; the rate itself gives activity.
  • Why blind faults: earthquakes occur on active structures whether or not a geologist has drawn them, so the signal is not conditioned on the catalogue at all.
  • Differs because: the organisers' 19-band stack contains one earthquake-density band; this uses the declustered variant and a directional operator, not a density.
  • Outcome: NEGATIVE and useful. Used as a hard selector it scores 0.0144 on the holdout instrument against 0.0391 for uniform random at the same budget — it is worse than random. Its single-feature AUC against held-out fault segments is 0.558 and 0.537 across the two folds: real but very weak (not leakage; the leakage threshold is 0.90). The general lesson is worth more than the hypothesis: concentrating dots on a weak ranking wastes coverage, and coverage is what this metric pays for.

H4 — Radiometric alteration corridors (rank 4, NOT RUN)

  • Layers: GeoDAWN K, U, Th, total count and upward-continued TMI at native resolution.
  • Signature: potassium enrichment and K/Th or U/Th ratios along fault-controlled fluid pathways; hydrothermal alteration changes the radiometric spectrum.
  • Why blind faults: alteration halos extend tens to hundreds of metres beyond a trace and survive burial of the scarp, so they can mark a fault that no one mapped.
  • Differs because: it uses ratios and not the channels.
  • Not run, with reason: the competition's 19 bands already include K, U, Th and total count, so the marginal information is the ratio only. Two programme lanes that built exactly these ratio detectors scored 0.0545 and 0.0963 live, well below the family best of 0.2778. Expected gain is low and the implementation cost is medium, so it was not worth one of this session's three experiments. Recorded as an honest negative-by-prior rather than silently dropped.

H3 — Structural extrapolation in front of a fault tip (rank 5, TESTED, NEGATIVE)

  • Layers: catalogue geometry only — tip position, strike, and the 3–10 px collar.
  • Signature: continue the trace along strike, close the gap between two en-echelon segments, and populate the relay bridge between overlapping tips.
  • Why blind faults: mapping is segment-wise, so linkage zones between two mapped segments are the most commonly unmapped part of a structure.
  • Differs because: it is the one hypothesis that needs no new data at all, and it is cheap enough to test decisively.
  • Outcome: NEGATIVE, 13× worse than random. The 3–10 px collar arm scores 0.0031 against 0.0405 for random at the same budget. This independently reproduces the two live results that motivated it and lost: gap closure (GEMSDOE27, 0.2449 against its parent's 0.2477) and tip step-over (GEMSDOE33, 0.2632 against the champion's 0.2778). The hypothesis is now closed with evidence rather than left open.
