Why GEMSDOE32 B2 scored 0.2778 — and what we did with that answer

B2 is an emission optimisation, not new geology. Byte-level forensics (raster restored and hashed here): 37,654 binary dots, minimum spacing 2.83 px — i.e. the old h19-5 multiline-corroboration surface emitted as a Poisson-disk D2.8 pattern with every dot within 2 px of the known-fault catalogue deleted. The 2.8-px spacing matches the 300 m triangular kernel of the DTI metric (α 0.2, β 0.8), so no two dots waste credit inside one kernel disk; the catalogue collar reallocates ~15% of the budget from already-known faults (which earn nothing) to off-catalogue targets. Diagnostic signature, measured on the shared evaluator: B2's hide-and-recover holdout is near zero (0.0045 — it deliberately avoids the catalogue) while its off-catalogue SGMC-CAL holdout is the family's highest (0.0641). Across the nine focus rasters SGMC-CAL tracks the owner-reported scores almost linearly (fit LB ≈ −0.0271 + 4.715·SGMC-CAL, RMSE 0.0023). Caution (Goodhart): submissions that chased SGMC faults directly scored 0.03–0.05 OWNER-REPORTED — the proxy is only valid for surfaces carrying real geological evidence.

Can it be beaten? Within this family, only marginally — the family ceiling is ~0.278 and the leaderboard high (OWNER-REPORTED 0.4413 / 0.3195, conflicting, unverified) implies someone is using richer physics (likely 1 m DEM + learned features). Our SDCC-1 candidate reaches SGMC-CAL 0.0624 [0.0511, 0.0745] — within CI of the family's best — while being measurably unique. Whether it beats 0.2778 is genuinely uncertain: the projection CI [0.2138, 0.3242] straddles it. That is the honest statement; no higher claim is supported by evidence.

Candidate hypotheses not yet implemented (ranked, for the next session)

  1. Channel-deflection / drainage-offset detector on the 1 m 3DEP DEM — targets piedmont faults under alluvium with no scarp or magnetic worm (the population every B2-family dot misses). Needs the official free USGS 3DEP 1 m source (registry.opendata.aws/usgs-lidar) — obtainable, but not from this sandbox (host-restricted). Highest expected DTI gain, highest cost.
  2. Radiometric alteration-halo detector (K-up/Th-flat) at 500–1000 m buffer, cross-gated by magnetic edges — partially covered by SDCC-1's ThK term; a dedicated halo model with the GeoDAWN K/Th/U bands (mirrored, SHA-pinned) is cheap to test next.
  3. Relay-ramp / step-over geometry completion on catalogue fault tips (mechanics-based infill between en-échelon segments) — moderate gain, cheap; must verify dots stay ≥65% distinct from GEMSDOE33's tip-stepover raster.
  4. Microseismicity lineaments (NCEDC/USGS ComCat epicentre density, 0–5 km depth) — free official data, unreachable from this sandbox; viability must be checked before proposing further.
  5. Learned fusion (gradient boosting) on the cached u8 stacks with fold-local training — replaces hand weights; medium cost; leakage-prone, needs strict OOF discipline.
