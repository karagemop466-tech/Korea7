San Francisco permit rules

Quotes are verbatim from the sources read on 2026-11-06. The implications are this addendum's reading of the text, not legal advice. The permit determination belongs to SF DBI.

P1Emergency work: permit within one daySF Plumbing Code §104.1 (second paragraph, as amended)
Emergency work for the protection of life or limb, health, property and public welfare shall have a permit obtained within one day of commencing such work, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays.
Applies to emergency work only. The brief does not describe an emergency. Whether any emergency condition exists is for the owner and DBI to decide.SF Plumbing Code 2025 (PDF), §104.1VERIFIED
P2Exempt: repair of leaksSF Plumbing Code §104.2(1)
Repair of leaks not requiring cutting into or removing piping.
The exemption is conditioned on no cutting into or removal of piping. Opening a wall or ceiling to reach the overflow does not clearly meet this condition.SF Plumbing Code 2025 (PDF), §104.2VERIFIEDA1
P3Exempt: unstopping traps, sewers, vents or waste pipesSF Plumbing Code §104.2(2)
Unstopping of traps, sewers, vents or waste pipes not requiring cutting into or removal of traps or piping.
Limited to unstopping. Replacing the waste and overflow assembly is not unstopping. This exemption does not appear to cover the described work.SF Plumbing Code 2025 (PDF), §104.2VERIFIEDA1
P4Exempt: faucets, angle stops, hose bibbs, roof drainSF Plumbing Code §104.2(3)
Replacement, repair or maintenance of faucets, angle stops, hose bibbs or roof drain not requiring cutting into or removal of piping.
Lists fixture types. A bathtub waste and overflow assembly is not named. Whether it counts as piping is a DBI determination. Treat it as permit-required until DBI confirms in writing.SF Plumbing Code 2025 (PDF), §104.2VERIFIEDA1
P5Who receives plumbing permitsSF Plumbing Code §104.3
A permit for inspection of the proposed plumbing work set forth in the application will be issued to State-licensed contractors or to homeowners subject to the requirements of Section 104.1.1.
Permits go to State-licensed contractors, or to owners under §104.1.1. The §104.1.1 owner path is limited to single-family dwellings. The brief does not say whether the building is single-family.SF Plumbing Code 2025 (PDF), §104.1.1 and §104.3VERIFIEDA6
P6Which code edition appliesSF DBI department page
If you file on or after January 1, 2026, you must use the 2025 California Codes and the 2025 San Francisco Code Amendments. There is no grace period.
Any filing made now uses the 2025 SF Plumbing Code cited above.SF DBI department pageVERIFIED
P7Permit portal changeSF DBI department page
Starting September 14, 2026, most over-the-counter construction permits will be available exclusively on the PermitSF portal.
Permit applications go through the PermitSF portal. The portal link on the DBI page was not opened for this build.SF DBI department pageVERIFIED
P8DBI closure and resumptionSF DBI department page (banner)
CLOSED Veterans Day 11 NOV. Permit Services and inspections will resume operations on Thursday, November 12, 2026.
Permit Services and inspections are closed on Wednesday 11 November 2026 and resume Thursday 12 November 2026. Plan calls accordingly.SF DBI department pageVERIFIED
P9Permit needed before cutting into or replacing pipessf.gov: Apply for a plumbing and mechanical permit (Requirements)
You need a permit before cutting into or replacing pipes, particularly pipes that will be covered by a wall or buried in the ground.
Stated directly by DBI's own service page. The overflow work is behind a wall, so this sentence applies on its face.sf.gov: Apply for a plumbing and mechanical permitVERIFIED
P10Contractor license types that can apply for a plumbing permitsf.gov: Apply for a plumbing and mechanical permit (Requirements)
Contractors must hold a C4, C16, C20, C36, C54, B, or A license to apply for a plumbing or mechanical permit in general. Your permit scope may be further limited depending on your license type.
A C-36 plumbing contractor, or a B holder within its scope, can apply. A license check (see licensing page) still applies.sf.gov: Apply for a plumbing and mechanical permitVERIFIED
P11Owner self-permit limitssf.gov: Apply for a plumbing and mechanical permit (Homeowners)
Only property owners of stand-alone single-family dwellings (R-3 occupancy) may apply for a plumbing or mechanical permit to do the work themselves.
Owner self-permit is limited to stand-alone single-family homes. Whether this unit qualifies is not stated in the brief.sf.gov: Apply for a plumbing and mechanical permit (Homeowners)VERIFIEDA6
P12R-2 apartment owners: drywall versus plumbingsf.gov: Apply for a kitchen or bath remodel permit (Special cases)
For R-2 apartment buildings, property owners can apply to do the construction work themselves for sheetrock, drywall, or cabinetry. They must hire contractors to do any electrical or plumbing work.
Under this sf.gov page, drywall can be applied for by an apartment owner, but plumbing must go to a contractor. This page is for kitchen and bath work; whether this scope fits is not confirmed.sf.gov: Apply for a kitchen or bath remodel permitVERIFIEDA18
P13Kitchen or bath permit scope limitsf.gov: Apply for a kitchen or bath remodel permit (What to know)
Apply online to renovate or replace kitchen or bath fixtures in-kind, with no structural work.
The online kitchen and bath permit is for in-kind work with no structural change. Opening a ceiling may take the job out of this path and into a building permit with plans. Confirm with DBI.sf.gov: Apply for a kitchen or bath remodel permitVERIFIEDA18

Licensing rules (CSLB) and the SF ECC list

Classification text is official. Seven license records are verified on CSLB (see the contractors page). Job fit and quotes are not verified.

IDRuleOfficial text (quoted)What it means hereSourceStatusFlags
L1C-36 Plumbing Contractor
A plumbing contractor provides a means for a supply of safe water, ample in volume and of suitable temperature for the purpose intended and the proper disposal of fluid waste from the premises in all structures and fixed works.
Plumbing work of this kind needs a C-36 holder.CSLB: C-36 - Plumbing ContractorVERIFIED
L2C-9 Drywall Contractor
A drywall contractor lays out and installs gypsum wall board and gypsum wall board assemblies including nonstructural metal framing members, and performs the taping and texturing operations.
Drywall ceiling or wall work is a C-9 scope. Drywall can also be done under a B license if the rules in L3 are met.CSLB: C-9 - Drywall ContractorVERIFIED
L3B General Building Contractor limits
A general building contractor shall not take a prime contract for any project involving trades other than framing or carpentry unless the prime contract requires at least two unrelated building trades or crafts other than framing or carpentry, or unless the general building contractor holds the appropriate license classification or subcontracts with an appropriately licensed specialty contractor to perform the work.
A B holder cannot take plumbing as a prime contract unless it holds C-36 or subcontracts a licensed C-36 contractor. This sets the licensing options for all-in-one.CSLB: B - General Building Contractor (Bus. & Prof. Code §7057(b))VERIFIEDA2
L4Checking a provider's license
Look up a contractor license or Home Improvement Salesperson (HIS) registration to verify information, including complaint disclosure.
The official lookup exists, but its result pages could not be retrieved from this environment. No provider license status is verified on this site.CSLB: Check a Contractor LicenseVERIFIEDA2
