How a trip-lever waste-and-overflow works

A trip-lever bathtub drain has no visible stopper in the drain opening. Instead, a lever on the overflow plate (the plate on the tub wall over the drain end of the overflow pipe) moves a linkage and a hollow plunger (drop cylinder) up and down inside the overflow pipe. Lever up drops the plunger into the junction of the overflow and the drain line to close the drain; lever down lifts it to open the drain. Watco trip-lever bath waste installation instructions (official manufacturer) diagrams the parts: overflow plate and screws, brass linkage with adjustment nut, drop cylinder/plunger, overflow elbow with gasket, tee, and drain elbow with strainer body under the tub. A second official sheet covers the linkage variants (Quick Adjust® / Slip Lock® / Turnstyle®).

Why trip levers stick — and why removal can fail

  • Buildup on the plunger: mineral (calcium/iron) deposits and gunk on the plunger make it bind against the inside of the overflow pipe (Plumbing Sniper trip-lever guide).
  • Corroded trim/linkage: the plate, screws, and brass linkage corrode and can freeze; a stuck or broken linkage sometimes has to be fished out or the assembly replaced (Hunker bathtub drain stopper guide).
  • All working parts are hidden: for plunger-type trip levers, everything that matters is inside the overflow pipe behind the wall — there is nothing to service at the drain opening itself (Hunker bathtub drain stopper guide).

Code context (California)

California's plumbing code is the California Plumbing Code (Title 24, Part 5), based on the Uniform Plumbing Code — official IAPMO readers: 2022 CPC · 2024 UPC. The UPC chapter on fixtures maps the relevant sections (titles from the official table of contents): 409.0 Bathtubs and Whirlpool Bathtubs, with 409.2 Waste Outlet, 409.3 Overflow, and 409.6 Installation and Access. In other words, the code family that governs this work has explicit provisions covering bathtub waste outlets, overflows, and installation/access — a professional bidding this job should be working from the current CPC edition.

License classes that fit this job (California)

  • C-36 Plumbing — the plumbing contractor classification (CSLB C-36 classification); covers the waste-and-overflow work itself. All eight candidates on the Contractors page hold active C-36 licenses.
  • B General Building — CSLB B classification. Under B&amp;P Code §7057 (official statutory text read 2026-11-06), a general building contractor's business involves structures whose construction requires “at least two unrelated building trades or crafts”, and a B contractor may take a prime contract for such a multi-trade project (holding the matching specialty classification or subcontracting where required). Plumbing + drywall = two unrelated trades, so a B + C-36 license holder can take this job as one contract including the ceiling opening and patch.
  • A C-36-only plumber can legally do the plumbing scope; the drywall opening/patch would then be a separate contract with a second licensed contractor (or the plumber's own licensed drywall subcontractor).
