The problem

The bathtub's wastewater overflow trip lever — the lever on the overflow plate that raises and lowers an internal plunger inside the overflow pipe — is stuck. The overflow assembly sits in a hard-to-access area behind the wall. In the field, removal from above has repeatedly failed: per the client, more than five licensed plumbers have been unable to remove it without concern over destroying the hidden plumbing behind the wall (client-stated). Those plumbers recommend working from below, but a storage unit occupies the space below, which may require opening the drywall ceiling to reach the hidden plumbing.

The unit is tenant-occupied, so the work must be planned and executed for a sensitive, in-use environment.

Work statement (as understood from the client's description)

  1. Remove or free the stuck trip-lever overflow assembly — or, if the hidden plumbing is compromised, replace the waste-and-overflow assembly.
  2. Obtain access: preferably from above (tub side); failing that, from below through the drywall ceiling above the storage area.
  3. Restore finishes: close and patch the ceiling (if opened) so the space below is left sound and finished.
  4. Do all of the above with licensed professionals, ideally one responsible party for plumbing and drywall, experienced with tenant-occupied units.
