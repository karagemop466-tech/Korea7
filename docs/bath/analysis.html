2 — Which scope fits the actual problem?

The client-stated field history is that 5+ licensed plumbers could not remove the assembly from above without risking the hidden plumbing (client-stated, unverified). If that stands after a re-inspection, then:

  • The $135 adjust scope and the $550 trim-replacement scope assume from-above removal works — exactly what the field history says keeps failing. Money spent there may not fix the tub.
  • The $614 full waste-and-overflow replacement is the scope aligned with the described failure — and it is plumbing-only: it does not include opening or patching the ceiling below.
  • Therefore the realistic total is $614 + access/patch work (or a re-bid on the same combined scope), not $135 or $550 alone. Angi's cost guide independently flags drywall removal as a major cost adder for exactly this kind of repair (source).

3 — The all-in-one requirement is a licensing question

The job's likely end-state — open the ceiling of the storage area, replace the waste-and-overflow from below, patch the ceiling — spans two unrelated trades: plumbing and drywall. California's Business & Professions Code §7057 (official text) defines the B General Building classification around exactly this case: a prime contract whose construction requires at least two unrelated trades. A C-36-only plumber cannot take the combined contract as a matter of licensing scope (CSLB C-36 classification).

5 — Decision checklist (before signing)

  1. Itemized written quote that names the access method (above vs. ceiling opening), the ceiling patch, and who does each part.
  2. Confirm the entity name on the quote matches the CSLB record (guard against the name-collision problem documented in the flags).
  3. Re-verify the license on CSLB Check a License that day; collect liability insurance certificates (and workers' comp where employees are used).
  4. References for tenant-occupied work (unverified criterion) plus a written dust/containment plan covering the occupied unit and the storage unit below.
  5. Agree in writing what happens on discovery (e.g., corroded pipe beyond the assembly): stop-work point, re-price procedure, and who patches if scope grows.
