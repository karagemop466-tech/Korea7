Purpose

Collect, organize, and analyze everything needed to hire help for one specific repair: a bathtub wastewater overflow trip lever that is stuck inside hidden plumbing behind a wall, in a tenant-occupied San Francisco unit, with a storage unit directly below. This page is the executive summary; every value on this site is source-linked, and anything that could not be confirmed from an official source is left blank or labeled client-provided and listed on the Flagged for Review page.

Situation (client-stated context)

  • The bathtub's trip-lever waste-and-overflow assembly is stuck; the working parts sit inside the overflow pipe hidden behind the wall.
  • Per the client, 5+ licensed plumbers have been unable to remove it from above without concern over destroying the hidden plumbing (client-stated; not independently verifiable — unverified context).
  • Recommended path from those plumbers: work from below — but a storage unit occupies the space below, so access may require opening the drywall ceiling.
  • The client wants an all-in-one service covering plumbing and drywall, with a licensed plumber or contractor experienced in delicate work in tenant-occupied units.

What was verified (2026-11-06)

  • 8 candidate contractors, each read directly from the official CSLB license-detail page: Expert Plumbing Solutions, Bay Area Plumbing, Fast Response Plumbing & Rooter, Ace Plumbing & Rooter, Genteel Plumbers, Atlas Plumbing & Rooter, Heise's Plumbing, Discount Plumbing Rooter Services.
  • All 8 licenses are current and active with C-36 Plumbing classification; two — Ace Plumbing & Rooter (829071) and Genteel Plumbers (1025814) — also hold B General Building on the same license.
  • The three companies behind the estimates were matched by name + exact address/phone to CSLB records; each match is listed on the Contractors page and flagged for client confirmation.
  • California statute B&P §7057 (official text): a prime contract requiring two unrelated trades (plumbing + drywall) is within the B classification — the legal basis for the all-in-one requirement.
  • Technical and code references pinned to official manufacturer instructions (Watco trip-lever installation PDF) and the California Plumbing Code / UPC (§409 Bathtubs: waste outlet, overflow, installation and access).

Core conclusion

If from-above removal has truly failed with 5+ licensed plumbers (client-stated), the $135 adjust and $550 trim-replacement scopes may not resolve the problem; the $614 full waste-and-overflow replacement scope is the one aligned with the described failure — and it covers plumbing only. The likely end-state is: open the ceiling below (drywall), replace the waste-and-overflow from below, then patch the ceiling. That is two unrelated trades, so the cleanest single-contract path is a contractor holding B + C-36 (Ace and Genteel Plumbers both hold this exact combination, verified on CSLB), or a C-36 plumber paired with a licensed drywall contractor under two contracts.

Recommended next steps

  1. Confirm with each quoting company which scope their price covers and get itemized written quotes (access/opening included or excluded — say which explicitly).
  2. Shortlist the two B + C-36 firms for single-contract bids; ask each for references from comparable tenant-occupied work (unverified criterion — cannot be verified from official sources).
  3. Re-check each license on CSLB Check a License on the day you hire, and collect insurance certificates (note the workers' compensation exemption flag on Bay Area Plumbing).
  4. Get the access plan in writing: where the opening will be cut, containment for the occupied unit and the storage unit below, and who patches/finishes.
