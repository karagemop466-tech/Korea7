Finding B · Access strategy drives cost and disruption.

From above (preferred)

Remove the overflow trip lever through the overflow plate opening without opening walls. Five-plus licensed plumbers reportedly could not do this without concern over damaging hidden plumbing. Lowest disruption if it succeeds; no storage-unit ceiling work.

From below (recommended by plumbers)

Access the waste-and-overflow plumbing through the ceiling of the storage unit below. Requires opening drywall, then repair/finish/paint. Plumbers uniformly recommend this route; an all-in-one plumbing + drywall vendor is preferred.

Screening criteria to apply when contacting plumbers.

  • Services San Francisco; experienced with overflow/trip-lever work where plumbing is hidden behind finished walls.
  • Willing to attempt removal from above first, then from below (storage-unit ceiling) if needed.
  • Can provide an all-in-one quote: plumbing + drywall repair + paint + disposal.
  • Experienced working in tenant-occupied units; will coordinate access with tenants.
  • CSLB license Active, C-36 plumbing classification, current bond, workers' comp — verified on cslb.ca.gov.
  • Can confirm whether a San Francisco plumbing permit applies to the final scope and who pulls it.
