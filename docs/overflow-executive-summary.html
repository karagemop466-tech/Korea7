Executive summary · Korea7 overflow project · 2026-11-06

Stuck bathtub overflow trip lever — what we know and what is needed

The bathtub wastewater overflow trip lever is stuck inside the waste-and-overflow plumbing behind the tub wall, in a hard-to-access area. Opening drywall may be required to reach the plumbing. This executive summary collects the problem statement, the three estimates supplied on 2026-08-30, access constraints, and the contractor search status. All figures are user-supplied and unverified.

1 · The problem.

What is stuck: the trip lever for the bathtub overflow drain — the lever on the overflow plate moves a linkage/plunger inside the overflow pipe that opens and closes the main drain. When the lever or plunger is stuck, the drain cannot be opened or closed from the overflow plate.

Access constraint: the overflow plumbing is hidden behind the bathtub wall and is hard to access. More than five licensed plumbers have been unable to remove the overflow from above without concern over destroying the hidden plumbing behind the wall. All of them recommend working from below.

Work from below: there is a storage unit below the tub, so accessing from below may require opening the drywall ceiling of that storage unit. The requester prefers an all-in-one service covering both plumbing and drywall.

Occupied unit: tenants occupy the unit where the trip lever is stuck inside the hidden plumbing. Work must be done in a sensitive, tenant-occupied environment; the contractor needs experience with delicate work in occupied units.

Preference: remove the overflow from above if possible; work from below (ceiling opening) only if above-access removal proves impossible.

📷

Pictures: the requester intends to supply pictures of the overflow plate and tub area as reference. None were included in this session; the description above is the working basis.

3 · What is needed to move forward.

Licensed plumber or contractor

Work should be performed by a California-licensed professional (California licensing exemption under B&P §7048 applies only below $1,000 and where no building permit is required — the “$500” figure widely quoted online is out of date; C-36 is the plumbing classification). License status, bond, and workers' compensation must be checked on the official CSLB Check a License tool before hiring.

San Francisco experience

Contractor should service San Francisco and have experience with this specific delicate work — overflow/trip-lever removal with hidden plumbing behind finished walls — in a tenant-occupied unit.

All-in-one plumbing + drywall

If work must proceed from below, the ceiling drywall of the storage unit below will need to be opened, repaired, and finished. One vendor handling both plumbing and drywall is preferred.

Review sources and flags

Contractor research was done on Yelp and company websites; license numbers come from company sites, BBB, and BuildZoom — none were re-verified on the official CSLB record in this session. See Contractors, Sources, and Flagged for review.
