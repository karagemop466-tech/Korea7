Executive summary

Decision brief · verified source snapshots and requester-reported information kept separate

The situation

The requester reports a stuck bathtub overflow trip-lever mechanism in a tenant-occupied San Francisco unit. Several licensed plumbers reportedly declined to force removal from above because of the risk to concealed plumbing. Access below may require opening the ceiling of a storage unit. The requester prefers above-tub removal where safe and wants one provider to coordinate plumbing and drywall.

These job details, including the number of prior plumbers, are unverified requester reports; no photos, inspection or original estimates were supplied for independent review.

Decision points

  1. Request a non-destructive assessment from above, with a stop point before force risks damaging hidden plumbing.
  2. If below access is needed, obtain a written plan for protecting stored property, opening the ceiling, testing the repair and restoring the finish.
  3. Ask for a single itemized quote covering the alternatives, exclusions, cleanup, schedule and tenant coordination. Existing quotes describe different scopes and are not directly comparable.
  4. Confirm license, insurance, specific experience, availability and compliance for the actual scope before booking.

San Francisco says a permit is needed before cutting into or replacing pipes, especially concealed pipes; exemptions depend on the work. See SF Department of Building Inspection guidance.
