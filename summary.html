1. The problem

The bathtub's waste-and-overflow trip lever is owner-provided stuck inside hidden plumbing behind the wall, in a hard-to-access area. More than five licensed plumbers reportedly declined to remove it from above because of the risk of damaging concealed plumbing, and recommended working from below — where a storage unit sits beneath the unit, so the drywall ceiling may need to be opened. The unit is tenant-occupied, so the owner wants one provider able to do both drywall and plumbing in a sensitive environment. Removing the overflow from above remains preferred if it can be done safely. None of these facts can be confirmed from a public source; see the Project brief and Flagged for Review page. Context: This Old House on below access, Angi on overflow function.

The three quotes describe three different scopes — an adjustment, a spout/lever replacement, and a full waste-and-overflow replacement. 5. Official rules (neutral)

SF.gov states: “You need a permit before cutting into or replacing pipes, particularly pipes that will be covered by a wall or buried in the ground” (sf.gov), and lists “minor repairs to existing interior plaster or wallboard” among work that does not need a permit (sf.gov). Contractors applying for a plumbing permit must hold C-4, C-16, C-20, C-36, C-54, B or A classifications (sf.gov). California B&P §7048 sets the current small-job licensing exemption at under $1,000 for casual/minor work that does not require a building permit, effective 2026-01-01 (leginfo). The hiring party should confirm applicability for the exact scope with SF DBI and the selected contractor. Full table: Permits & licensing.
