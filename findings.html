Finding 1 — When above-access fails, below-access is the documented route

The owner reports 5+ licensed plumbers declined to remove the overflow from above for fear of damaging the hidden assembly owner-provided. The trade literature matches that pattern:

  • Replacing the full waste-and-overflow piping “usually requires access — either from an access panel, a ceiling below, or an open wall behind the tub,” while plate/gasket work can sometimes be done from the tub side only. — Horow overflow guide (page dated 2026-01-05)
  • This Old House's replacement guide states plainly: “For stubborn drains, you may need to access the plumbing from below the tub,” and in extreme cases the old drain is cut out from below. — This Old House (2026-06-01)
  • For a second-floor tub, professionals “often access the drain by making a small, clean cut in the drywall of the ceiling directly below the tub — drywall is significantly easier and cheaper to repair than custom tile.” — The Overall Plumber (2026-09-10)
✅

Conclusion: the below-access plan (storage-unit ceiling) is consistent with standard practice when front access fails — not a unusual workaround. Ask the plumber to confirm the specific cut location and size in writing before work starts.

Finding 5 — Planning work in a tenant-occupied unit

  • Duration: with clear access (panel or ceiling below), a complete waste-and-overflow replacement takes about 2–4 hours; cutting drywall or tight crawlspaces push it to 4+ hours. — The Overall Plumber (2026-09-10)
  • Leak test is part of the job: the standard professional stress test plugs the drain, fills the bathtub to check the drain and overflow connections, then releases the water and inspects every joint and gasket under flow conditions — meaning the space below/behind must stay accessible during the test, not just during the repair. A complete trip-lever waste-and-overflow kit is a single coordinated assembly (manufacturer product page). — The Overall Plumber (2026-09-10) · Oatey (manufacturer)
  • Sequencing for tenants: the same procedure (adjust, reinsert, test with water, repeat until watertight) is standard — confirm the plumber will complete the water test before closing any ceiling. — This Old House (2026-05-27)
  • Occupied-unit constraints (water off, noise, debris) and the below-access route itself are owner-provided owner-provided — confirm scope, containment, and schedule directly with the chosen contractor.
