Technical background — bathtub waste-and-overflow trip lever

Topic What the source states Relevance to this job Verification Source
Trip-lever mechanism The trip lever operates a hidden linkage behind the tub wall connected to the stopper; diagnosis starts by removing the overflow cover plate and inspecting the links Explains why a seized linkage is not visible from the tub side verified S21 · S17
Bath waste-and-overflow parts Drain shoe, linkage assembly, overflow plate and trap form the system; the overflow plate is a safety feature Defines the concealed assembly at issue verified S18 · S17
Above-access removal Remove the two screws in the overflow plate and pull the plate away; the upper linkage should follow Supports the preferred from-above approach when the linkage is free verified S18 · S20
Stuck / corroded drains For stubborn drains, access the plumbing from below the tub; extreme cases may require cutting out old drain components from below Explains why five-plus plumbers recommended below access verified S18
Below-access assembly Installing a new kit requires aligning the rubber gasket and drain elbow from below while a helper holds the shoe from above Concealed work usually needs an opening or access panel verified S18
Replacement kit Oatey publishes a full 'Dearborn Plastic Tubular Trip-Lever Bath Waste' kit with a chrome-finish stopper Confirms the component type is a standard manufactured assembly manufacturer S14
Overflow function The overflow directs excess water into a concealed channel connected to the drain; traditional and integral types exist Distinguishes the overflow plate from the trip-lever linkage verified S17
Testing after replacement Trade guidance describes a hydrostatic / fill-and-inspect test of the drain and overflow connections Plan access to the space below for a water test verified S22
